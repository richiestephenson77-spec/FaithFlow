What the borders mean

Territory shapes are hand-authored, illustrative regional footprints. They are not surveyed or scholarly reconstructed borders, and do not show every state, enclave or degree of control. Some overlays combine direct rule, client kingdoms and spheres of influence.

What the animation means

Dragging blends neighboring scenes. Borders can move and colors and labels can fade. Intermediate shapes are a presentation effect—not evidence of what a country looked like in that particular year. Turn off Animate to inspect individual snapshots.

Names and dates

Place names are selected recognizable labels, not a complete historical gazetteer. City markers indicate story relevance, not founding or abandonment. Patriarchal, Exodus and united-monarchy chronology and geography remain debated. The first scenes are an ancient-world prologue.

Map and sources

The land silhouette is Natural Earth public-domain data, showing a modern coastline reference. Each scene links to historical background. None of those links validates the illustrative polygons. A publication-grade historical atlas needs a separately reviewed, licensed boundary dataset.
